## Phase A — `async_logic_design/` · activity: **re-generate**

**Scope.** 8 static-CMOS cells × 3 voltage domains (24 cells). Re-ran the generator chain
(`async_lib.py` decks → `async_run.py` sizing/PVT) under the Step-0 contract, regenerated
`results.json`, `REPORT.md`, `SUMMARY.md`. Stamp: `model=v2-grounded ngspice=ngspice-45`.

**Step-0 decision 1 applied.** Input-cap contract relaxed **5.0 → 6.5 fF hard / 6.0 fF sizing
target** (`CAP_HARD`, `CAP_MODEL_TGT` in `async_run.py`). Old contract was met with 0.022 fF margin
(worst pin NOR2 = 4.978 fF against a 5.0 fF wall).

**Key finding — the contract, not the raw cap number, is what F6 moved.** The input-pin capacitance
is a *gate* load; the F6 BSIM3 junction caps (`cj=0.0018`, `cjsw=1e-10` on NMOS18/…) sit on
drain/source diffusions and load the **output**, not the input gate. Re-measuring the *input* cap at
fixed sizing therefore reproduces the old numbers to <0.01 fF — the pre-registered "worst pins above
5 fF" does not appear as a raw-fF jump. What F6 actually moves is (a) **V_M centring** for the cells
whose PMOS had to be starved to stay under 5 fF (NOR2/OR2), and (b) **output-loaded rise/fall**. The
6.5 fF relaxation lets NOR2/OR2 reach their ideal P/N ratio: **`cap_limited` flips True→False in all
three domains** and the V_M envelope's low end lifts (5v0 NOR2 43→45 %Vdd).

**Rise/fall (net effect).** Old-vs-new t_fall is the *net* of two opposing changes: F6 junction caps
add real output load (slower), while the relaxed budget allows ~30 % wider devices (faster). Net:
the series-PMOS / 2-high cells (NOR2/OR2/XOR2/XNOR2) slow **+45–60 %** because the junction load
dominates their weak pull path; simpler cells stay within **±10 %**. (Isolation check: INV 1v8 at
*old* sizing on the new models is +21 % on t_fall; after the resize it is +3 % net.)
